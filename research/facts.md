## 2. India's 2023–2025 shutdowns, broken down

- **2023:** Shutdowns hit 13 states and union territories. 59% targeted only mobile networks, and over 41% lasted five days or more, up from 15% in 2022. Source: [Access Now](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/india-keepiton-internet-shutdowns-2023-en/)
- **2024:** Shutdowns hit 16 states and UTs. Manipur had 21, Haryana 12 and Jammu & Kashmir 12. 41 were linked to protests and 23 to communal violence. Source: [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1079557/scroll_in)
- **2022:** At least 49 of India's 84 shutdowns were in Jammu & Kashmir. Source: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/2/28/in-2022-the-world-saw-187-internet-shutdowns-84-by-india-alone)
- **2021:** 85 of India's 106 shutdowns were in Jammu & Kashmir. Source: [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1022854/scroll_in)

## 4. Jammu & Kashmir had 4G mobile data cut for about 550 days

- Mobile internet was cut on 4–5 Aug 2019.
- 4G was restored on a trial basis in Ganderbal and Udhampur on 16 Aug 2020.
- The rest of J&K stayed on 2G until the Home Department restored 4G UT-wide on 5 Feb 2021. That is 18 months, or about 550 days.
- Access Now counted the first phase, a complete internet blackout, as 175 days long. It was the second-longest shutdown worldwide in 2019.
- Sources: [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy-politics/story/4g-internet-services-restored-in-jammu-and-kashmir-286694-2021-02-05), [Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2020/03/02/internet-shutdowns-in-2019-india-continued-to-top-list-of-worst-offenders/)

## 5. Manipur 2023 was the world's longest shutdown that year

## 9. *Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India* (Supreme Court, 10 Jan 2020)

The Court held:
- Speech and trade carried out over the internet are protected under Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g). Restrictions must pass the proportionality test.
- An order suspending the internet indefinitely is impermissible under the 2017 Suspension Rules. Suspensions must be temporary and last no longer than necessary.
- A complete suspension must be necessary and unavoidable, and less intrusive alternatives must be considered first.
- Suspension orders are open to judicial review, and the State must produce them. The Court disfavoured secret orders.
- Because the Rules had no periodic review, the Review Committee must review every order within **seven working days** of its previous review.

Sources: [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/freedoms-of-speech-expression-trade-commerce-internet-constitutionally-protected-sc-kashmir-case-151488), [Indian Kanoon full text](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/82461587/), [Columbia Global Freedom of Expression](https://globalfreedomofexpression.columbia.edu/cases/bhasin-v-union-of-india)

- Follow-up: on 30 Jan 2024, in the Foundation of Media Professionals plea, the Supreme Court said review committee orders must be published, though their internal deliberations need not be. Source: [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/01/223-sc-orders-internet-bans-published-anuradha-bhasin-guidelines/)
- Note: sources name the Bhasin bench differently. The judgment text gives Ramana, Subhash Reddy and Gavai, JJ.

## 10. The 15-day cap first arrived in November 2020

- On 10 Nov 2020, the Centre inserted Rule 2A into the 2017 Suspension Rules. It limits each suspension order to no more than 15 days in operation.
- The amendment did not require publication of orders or periodic review.
- Source: [Internet Freedom Foundation](https://internetfreedom.in/telecom-suspension-rules-amendment-15-day-time-limit/)

## 11. The Telecommunications (Temporary Suspension of Services) Rules, 2024 (G.S.R. 724(E), 22 Nov 2024)

The Rules were made under s.20 of the Telecommunications Act, 2023. They replaced the 2017 Rules and took effect on publication. What the text says:

- **Who orders:** the Union Home Secretary, or the State Home Secretary (Rule 2(1)(b)). Reasons must be recorded in writing (Rule 3(1)).
- **Emergency orders:** under "unavoidable circumstances", an officer of at least Joint Secretary rank to the Central Government may issue an order. It must be confirmed by the competent authority "within twenty-four hours", failing which it "shall cease to exist" (Rule 3(1), provisos).
- **Content and limits:** every order "shall be published". It must clearly state its reasons, be limited to a clearly defined area and service type, and run for "a specified duration, not exceeding fifteen days" (Rule 3(2)).
- **Review:** a copy of the order goes to the review committee within 24 hours (Rule 3(3)). The committee must "meet within five days" and may set the order aside (Rule 5(3)-(4)).
- **Last resort:** an order may be made only if the objectives "cannot be achieved by any other reasonable means" (Rule 3(4)).
- **What the Rules do not say:** they do not prescribe how orders are published. The 15-day cap applies per order, so orders can be renewed back to back.

Sources: [Gazette PDF, DoT](https://eservices.dot.gov.in/sites/default/files/circular-notifications/temporary-suspension-of-services-rules-2024.pdf), [IFF first read](https://internetfreedom.in/first-read-telecom-suspension-rules-2024/)

- Conflict: IFF says the review timeline counts working days. The notified English text says only "within five days".

## 12. Parliament's IT committee called shutdowns routine policing (26th Report, 1 Dec 2021)

The Standing Committee on Communications and IT ("Suspension of Telecom Services/Internet and its Impact") found:
- States were ordering shutdowns for routine policing and administrative aims, such as stopping exam cheating, with no clear parameters for deciding when one is warranted.
- DoT had no data on whether review committees even existed in every state.
- Neither DoT nor MHA had studied the economic impact of shutdowns.

It recommended a centralised database of every shutdown, recording its duration, reasons and authority. It also recommended adding non-official members to review committees.

Sources: [PRS summary](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/suspension-of-telecom-services-internet-and-its-impact), [IFF](https://internetfreedom.in/concerned-with-frequent-internet-suspensions-parliamentary-committee-recommends-an-overhaul/)

## 13. The Union government still keeps no count of state shutdowns

- In its Action Taken Report of Feb 2023, the committee recorded that DoT and MHA had made no effort to keep records of shutdowns ordered by states. Both ministries said they are "not aware of the number of internet shutdowns imposed by the States."
- Source: [MediaNama, 9 Feb 2023 report](https://www.medianama.com/2023/03/223-7-recommendations-internet-shutdowns-telecom-dept/)
- The official Lok Sabha PDF is listed at eparlib.nic.in (17_Communications_and_Information_Technology_37.pdf). That host could not be reached from our fetcher, so cite MediaNama.

## 14. Orders often go unpublished

- Access Now says Indian officials "fail to publish shutdown orders". Source: [Access Now 2023](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/india-keepiton-internet-shutdowns-2023-en/)
- IFF filed RTIs on state review committees and says it received no information about whether any had been constituted. Source: [IFF](https://www.internetfreedom.in/shutdowns-report-dot-mha/)
- This gap is what Switched Off fills: a public, timestamped register of every order.
